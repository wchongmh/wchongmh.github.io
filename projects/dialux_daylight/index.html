Daylight and Lighting Design

Glasgow Kirkintilloch Residential Project

Lighting Design Dialux Daylight Factor Calculations BS EN 17037 SLL Code Compliance Daylight Autonomy Energy Efficiency

This project analyzes daylight performance in a newly constructed three-bedroom semi-detached house in Glasgow, comparing manual Daylight Factor calculations with Dialux simulations to optimize natural lighting while meeting UK building standards.

House Overview

3D Floor Plan

The project evaluates daylight performance across 11 zones in a residential property at Market Road, Kirkintilloch, Glasgow (G66 3JL). Using both manual calculations and Dialux simulations, we assessed compliance with BS EN 17037 standards and identified opportunities for improving natural lighting distribution while considering energy efficiency.

1 Project Overview

The daylight analysis covers eight primary spaces across two floors, evaluating both quantitative metrics (Daylight Factor, illuminance levels) and qualitative aspects (light distribution, glare potential). The methodology combines traditional manual calculations with advanced computer simulations to validate results.

Daylight Factor Calculation Steps

  1. Identify zones with window openings
  2. Measure room and window dimensions
  3. Determine visible sky angle considering obstructions
  4. Calculate window-to-floor area ratio
  5. Compute average reflectance of room surfaces
  6. Apply Daylight Factor formula

Daylight Factor Formula:

DFave = (Ag × θ × T) / [A × (1 - R2)]

Where:
Ag = Window area
θ = Visible sky angle
T = Transmittance factor
A = Total room surface area
R = Average reflectance

Daylight Factor calculations were performed manually for each room with window openings, considering room dimensions, window properties, surface reflectances, and external obstructions. The following examples demonstrate the calculation methodology.

  • Daylight linking for kitchens and workspaces
  • Motion sensors for hallways and bathrooms
  • Soft-start ballasts for fluorescent lighting

12 Recommendations

Based on the analysis, several recommendations are proposed to improve daylight utilization, enhance safety, and optimize energy efficiency throughout the property.

⚠️

Safety Improvements

  • Install anti-slip measures in low-DF areas
  • Add supplemental lighting in threshold areas
  • Consider motion-activated lighting for safety
🪟

Daylight Enhancement

  • Implement light shelves in south-facing rooms
  • Use higher reflectance surfaces in low-DF areas
  • Consider daylight redirecting films
⚡

Lighting System Upgrades

  • Implement automated lighting controls system-wide
  • Upgrade to energy-efficient luminaires
  • Install task lighting in workspaces

13 Conclusion

The daylight and lighting design analysis for this Glasgow residential property demonstrates that most rooms meet or exceed recommended daylight factor standards. The project highlights:

Key Findings

  • North-facing rooms benefit from consistent diffuse light but require careful design to achieve adequate illumination
  • Automatic lighting controls could reduce energy consumption by over 25%
  • Several safety-critical areas were identified needing supplemental lighting
  • Bedroom 1 performed best while cloakroom performed worst in daylight metrics

Key Strengths

  • Bedroom 1 achieves excellent daylight performance (16.28% DF manual, 3.79% DF Dialux)
  • Kitchen and dining areas benefit from south-facing orientation
  • Automatic controls can save 225 kWh annually

Areas for Improvement

  • Cloakroom and some threshold areas require supplemental lighting
  • Safety concerns identified in low-DF transition areas
  • Opportunities for enhanced daylight redirection

Final Outcome

The comprehensive analysis combining manual calculations with Dialux simulations provides a robust foundation for optimizing both natural and artificial lighting in the property. The recommendations address safety concerns while maximizing energy efficiency and occupant comfort.

Key Takeaways

  • Most rooms met minimum 2% DF requirement, but illuminance levels often fell short of recommendations
  • Significant variation between manual and Dialux results highlights value of simulation tools
  • Automatic lighting controls offer substantial energy savings (225 kWh annually)
  • Targeted improvements could enhance both daylight performance and safety

Project Reflections

This comprehensive analysis demonstrated the value of combining manual calculations with simulation tools for robust daylight evaluation. While the property generally meets basic requirements, opportunities exist to optimize both daylight utilization and electric lighting efficiency through strategic design interventions.
