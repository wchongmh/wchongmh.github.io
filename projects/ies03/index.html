03 Standards & Requirements

The design follows UK lighting standards including BS EN 17037 for daylight and BS EN 12464-1 for illuminance levels. Key requirements include:

Daylight Factors

  • Minimum 2% average DF for daylit ambiance
  • 5% average DF for daylight autonomy

Illuminance Levels

  • Living rooms: ≥200 lux
  • Bedrooms: ≥100 lux
  • Kitchens: ≥500 lux
  • Bathrooms: ≥200 lux

Material Specifications

Reflectance values were carefully selected according to SLL Code for Lighting 2022:

  • Ceiling reflectance: 0.8 (70-90% range)
  • Wall reflectance: 0.65 (50-80% range)
  • Floor reflectance: 0.4 (20-60% range)
  • Window glazing reflectance: 0.1

Dirt correction factor of 0.9 was applied for urban setting.

04 Methodology

The analysis employed two complementary approaches to evaluate daylight performance:

Manual Calculations

Daylight Factor equations considering:

  • Room geometry and window dimensions
  • Surface reflectance values
  • External obstructions
  • Glasgow's overcast sky conditions

Dialux Simulations

Comprehensive 3D modeling providing:

  • Point-by-point daylight analysis
  • Surface daylight factors
  • Illuminance distribution
  • Energy consumption estimates

Daylight Factor Calculation Steps

  1. Identify zones with window openings
  2. Measure room and window dimensions
  3. Determine visible sky angle considering obstructions
  4. Calculate window-to-floor area ratio
  5. Compute average reflectance of room surfaces
  6. Apply Daylight Factor formula

Daylight Factor Formula:

DFave = (Ag × θ × T) / [A × (1 - R2)]

Where:
Ag = Window area
θ = Visible sky angle
T = Transmittance factor
A = Total room surface area
R = Average reflectance

05 Results

The analysis revealed varying daylight performance across different rooms, with most spaces meeting minimum requirements but showing opportunities for optimization.

Manual vs. Dialux Comparison

Key Findings:

  • Bedroom 1 performed best (16.28% manual, 3.79% Dialux)
  • Cloakroom performed worst (0.76% manual, 0.72% Dialux)
  • Bathroom only failed manual calculation (0.66%)
  • Significant differences between methods due to Dialux's detailed modeling

Illuminance Levels

Compliance Analysis:

  • Only Bedroom 1 met recommended illuminance levels (158 lux)
  • Kitchen fell short of 500 lux requirement (96 lux)
  • Living areas averaged 101 lux (below 150 lux target)

06 Energy Analysis

Comparison of manual control versus automatic/daylight-dependent lighting systems revealed significant energy savings potential.

Key Findings:

  • Total annual savings: 225.02 kWh (27% reduction)
  • Kitchen/dining showed largest difference (45.05 kWh)
  • Bedroom 2 showed smallest difference (11.8 kWh)
  • Automatic controls most effective in frequently used spaces

Recommended Control Strategies

Based on SLL Lighting Guide 9 recommendations:

  • Combined photocell and presence sensors in living areas
  • Daylight linking for kitchens and workspaces
  • Motion sensors for hallways and bathrooms
  • Soft-start ballasts for fluorescent lighting

07 Recommendations

⚠️

Safety Improvements

  • Install anti-slip strips in bathroom/shower areas
  • Add motion-activated lighting in transition areas
  • Improve lighting at door thresholds and steps
🪟

Daylight Enhancement

  • Consider light shelves for deeper light penetration
  • Evaluate window enlargement in poor-performing rooms
  • Use daylight redirecting film on select windows
💡

Lighting System Upgrades

  • Implement automated lighting controls system-wide
  • Upgrade to energy-efficient luminaires
  • Install task lighting in workspaces

08 Conclusion

The daylight and lighting design analysis for this Glasgow residential property demonstrates that most rooms meet or exceed recommended daylight factor standards. The project highlights:

Key Strengths

  • Bedroom 1 achieves excellent daylight performance (16.28% DF manual, 3.79% DF Dialux)
  • Kitchen and dining areas benefit from south-facing orientation
  • Automatic controls can save 225 kWh annually

Areas for Improvement

  • Cloakroom and some threshold areas require supplemental lighting
  • Safety concerns identified in low-DF transition areas
  • Opportunities for enhanced daylight redirection

Final Outcome

The comprehensive analysis combining manual calculations with Dialux simulations provides a robust foundation for optimizing both natural and artificial lighting in the property. The recommendations address safety concerns while maximizing energy efficiency and occupant comfort.

Key Takeaways

  • Most rooms met minimum 2% DF requirement, but illuminance levels often fell short of recommendations
  • Significant variation between manual and Dialux results highlights value of simulation tools
  • Automatic lighting controls offer substantial energy savings (225 kWh annually)
  • Targeted improvements could enhance both daylight performance and safety

Project Reflections

This comprehensive analysis demonstrated the value of combining manual calculations with simulation tools for robust daylight evaluation. While the property generally meets basic requirements, opportunities exist to optimize both daylight utilization and electric lighting efficiency through strategic design interventions.
