This project demonstrates the comprehensive energy compliance assessment for a two-story office building in Glasgow, analyzing and optimizing the building's performance to meet Section 6 of the Scottish Technical Standards (2015-2023).

Design process in IES VE software

3D view of the office building

The project involved detailed energy modeling of a 1,182.07 m² office building in Glasgow (ASHRAE Climate Zone 5A) using IES VE Compliance software. Initial analysis revealed significant non-compliance with Section 6 standards, requiring comprehensive modifications to building fabric, HVAC systems, lighting, and incorporation of renewables to achieve compliance.

01 Project Overview

The building initially failed to meet Section 6 requirements with energy consumption of 232.48 kWh/m² (vs 53.56 kWh/m² limit) and CO₂ emissions of 98.5 kgCO₂/m² (vs 50 kgCO₂/m² limit). Through systematic improvements to air permeability, U-values, HVAC systems, lighting efficiency, and renewable integration, the project achieved full compliance with final emissions of 12.2 kgCO₂/m².

03 Compliance Requirements

Section 6 of the Scottish Technical Standards establishes rigorous energy performance criteria for office buildings, including maximum CO₂ emissions, thermal performance standards, and system efficiency requirements.

Key Standards

Air Permeability

Required value of 5 m³/hr/m² at 50 Pa for non-domestic buildings (EN 15242)

Building Fabric

U-values calculated per BS EN ISO 6946:2007 with thermal bridge considerations

HVAC Systems

EN 15243 defines SCoP for heating and SSEER for cooling efficiency metrics

Lighting

Illuminance levels and power density requirements per NCM Activity Database

04 Initial Analysis

The initial energy simulation revealed significant non-compliance across multiple parameters, with CO₂ emissions nearly double the allowable limit and energy consumption over four times the target.

Performance Metrics

  • Energy Consumption: 232.48 kWh/m² (vs 53.56 limit)
  • CO₂ Emissions: 98.5 kgCO₂/m² (vs 50 limit)
  • Average U-value: 0.83 W/m²K (vs 0.27 recommended)
  • Air Permeability: 10 m³/hr/m² (vs 5 required)

System Efficiency

Original HVAC systems showed poor efficiency:

  • Dual-duct VAV in offices and circulation areas
  • Induction systems in eating/drinking areas
  • No heat recovery mechanisms

Fabric Performance

Building fabric significantly underperformed Section 6 guidance:

  • External walls: 0.27 W/m²K (actual) vs 0.21 required
  • Internal partitions: 1.79 W/m²K vs 0.44 required
  • Exposed floors: 0.22 W/m²K vs 0.17 required

Component Analysis

Detailed review of all building elements revealed multiple opportunities for improvement in:

  • Wall constructions
  • Floor assemblies
  • Roof systems
  • Glazing performance

05 Building Fabric Improvements

Comprehensive upgrades to the building envelope achieved the largest single reduction in CO₂ emissions (22.8 kgCO₂/m²) through improved insulation and reduced thermal bridging.

Material Upgrades

  • External walls: Added insulation (95mm) and changed sheathing
  • Internal partitions: Added 50mm insulation and reduced cavity
  • Exposed floors: Increased insulation to 120mm
  • Roof: Added polyurethane board (74.9mm)

Performance Impact

Fabric improvements alone reduced:

  • BER from 97.9 to 75.1 kgCO₂/m²
  • U-values met or exceeded all Section 6 requirements
  • Air permeability improved to 5 m³/hr/m²

06 HVAC System Optimization

The HVAC system redesign focused on zone-specific solutions incorporating biomass energy sources and heat recovery, achieving a 17.7 kgCO₂/m² reduction in BER.

System Upgrades

Fan Coil Units

Replaced dual-duct VAV and induction systems in offices and eating areas:

  • ECM motors for variable speed control
  • Both heating and cooling capability
  • Precise zone temperature control
  • 17.7 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction

Radiator Circuits

Implemented in changing facilities and toilets:

  • Biomass-fueled LTHW boiler (75% efficiency)
  • Optimum start/stop controls
  • Weather compensation
  • 9.7 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction

Dual-duct VAV

Optimized for plant rooms and cupboards:

  • Thermal wheel heat recovery (75% efficiency)
  • Class D ductwork leakage standard
  • Specific fan power of 1.5 W/(l/s)
  • 15.5 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction

Local Electric Heaters

For toilets and changing rooms:

  • Biomass heat source
  • SFP reduced from 2.60 to 1.10
  • Class L1 AHU air leakage standard
  • 15.5 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction

Domestic Hot Water

  • High-efficiency air source heat pump (91% seasonal efficiency)
  • Biogas-fueled with optimum start/stop controls
  • Designed for peak occupancy periods
  • 0.4 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction

07 Lighting Optimization

The lighting system redesign achieved a 67% reduction in power density while maintaining adequate illuminance levels throughout the building.

System Upgrades

  • Power density reduced from 18.75 W/m² to 7 W/m²
  • Office areas: Photoelectric dimming with back sensors
  • Other areas: Manual switching
  • Stand-alone sensors with 0.2 W/m² parasitic power

Performance Impact

  • 12.5 kgCO₂/m² reduction in BER
  • Maintained required illuminance levels
  • Significant reduction in energy consumption
  • Improved visual comfort for occupants

08 Renewable Energy Integration

The final 6.2 kgCO₂/m² reduction needed for compliance was achieved through strategic integration of solar photovoltaic technology.

Solar PV System

  • 4 monocrystalline panels (60 m² each)
  • South-facing at 30° inclination
  • Minimum yield of 850 kWh/kWp/year
  • Compliant with IEC 61215 and 61730 standards
  • 6.2 kgCO₂/m² BER reduction
