AXA Center, 151 Gloucester Road

Building Systems AutoCAD MEP Hydraulic Calculations BS EN 12845 Fire Safety Ordinance IOP Method BIM Coordination

The AXA Center project features integrated plumbing and fire protection systems for a 30-story commercial office tower in Wan Chai. The design addresses Hong Kong's stringent building codes while optimizing for maintenance accessibility and system redundancy.

Building exterior showing service riser locations

Map location of AXA Center, 151 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai

This 30-story commercial office tower, standing 123.85 meters tall with typical floor plates of 717.6 m², presents distinct challenges for vertical water distribution and fire protection. To address these, the design incorporates zoned systems with pressure management to ensure consistent performance across all levels. The system complies with Hong Kong's Fire Services Ordinance, Water Supplies Department requirements, BS EN 12845, and relevant local building regulations.

1 Project Overview

The plumbing infrastructure serves 30 floors of office space with male, female, and disabled toilet facilities on each level. The fire protection system is designed to OH3 hazard classification with three sprinkler zones to accommodate the 123.85m building height.

6 Project Reflection

This project successfully integrated academic knowledge with practical design requirements for high-rise commercial buildings. The comprehensive systems design addressed all regulatory requirements while optimizing for maintainability and future flexibility. The AXA Centre project presented unique challenges in vertical distribution and pressure management for both plumbing and fire protection systems. Key lessons learned:

Key Lessons

  • Practical application of BS EN 12845 and Hong Kong local building codes
  • Importance of pressure management in high-rise buildings
  • Benefits of standardized sprinkler layouts for future flexibility
  • Benefits of zoned systems for fire protection
  • Considerations for seawater flush systems
  • Value of early BIM coordination for service ducts and complex service integrations
  • Need for redundant pumping systems in critical applications
