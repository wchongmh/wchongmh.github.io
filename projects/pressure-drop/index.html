1 Introduction

The HM 150.11 module features a 10-meter closed-loop system with 1-inch diameter steel pipes, centrifugal pump, magnetic flow meter, and various piping components. Pressure gauges were strategically placed before and after each component to measure localized pressure drops.

Experimental Methodology

The experimental procedure followed a rigorous three-phase approach to ensure accurate measurement of pressure drops across various piping components under controlled flow conditions.

Step 1

System Preparation

Assembled piping system with all components and calibrated measurement instruments. Filled system with water at 20°C (ρ=998.2 kg/m³).

Step 2

Flow Rate Adjustment

Gradually increased flow rate from 0.2 to 2 m/s using variable frequency drive, maintaining steady-state conditions at each increment.

Step 3

Data Collection

Recorded pressure differentials across each component and corresponding flow rates using magnetic flow meter and pressure gauges.

Step 4

Data Analysis

Calculated local pressure drops using: ΔpM = (ρv²)/2 and determined resistance coefficients for each component.

Local Pressure Drop Calculation

ΔpM = ζ × ½ρV2

Where:
ΔpM = Local pressure drop (Pa)
ζ = Loss coefficient
ρ = Fluid density (kg/m³)
V = Flow velocity (m/s)

4 Engineering Insights

Key Findings

  • Control valve showed highest pressure drop (up to 2402.63 Pa at 0.367 L/s)
  • 90° elbows showed highest ζ coefficients (avg 3.5)
  • 90° elbows demonstrated consistent ζ values between 3.3-4.0
  • Elbows and reducers contribute significantly to energy consumption
  • Pressure drops increased quadratically with flow velocity
  • Linear pressure drop-flow relationship confirmed
  • Control valve contributed to 35% of total system pressure loss
  • Control valves account for >60% of system pressure loss

Design Implications

  • Minimize 90° elbows in high-flow systems
  • Use gradual bends (ζ = 2.8-3.1) instead of sharp elbows
  • Size pumps for worst-case pressure drop scenarios

5 Conclusions & Recommendations

The experiment successfully quantified pressure losses in various piping components, providing valuable data for fluid system design. The HM 150.11 module proved effective for precise measurements, though several improvements could enhance future studies:

📊

Experimental Summary

  • Control valves account for 45-60% of total system pressure drop
  • Elbows contribute significantly to energy losses in piping networks
  • Straight pipe sections show minimal pressure loss
  • Experimental data validates theoretical predictions
🛠️

Design Implications

  • Minimize unnecessary fittings in high-flow systems
  • Consider gradual diameter changes instead of sudden transitions
  • Proper valve selection critical for energy efficiency
  • Accurate ζ values enable better pump sizing
💻

Future Enhancements

  • Incorporate ultrasonic flow measurement
  • Test with variable fluid viscosities
  • Integrate with CFD software for simulation-experiment correlation
  • Incorporate machine learning for real-time flow prediction
  • Expand component library (e.g., different valve types)
